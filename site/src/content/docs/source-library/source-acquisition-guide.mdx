This guide is for readers, librarians, engineers, historians, and independent researchers who find a Steinmetz book, paper, lecture, patent, diagram, archive record, or scan that should belong in the archive.

The short rule is simple: **bring the source into custody before asking the archive to decode it.** A title, a rumor, a bibliography entry, or an unsourced quotation can be useful as a lead, but it is not yet a decoded source.

## What Counts As Source Custody

A source is in usable custody when the archive can point to a stable object that a future reviewer can inspect again.

## Metadata To Submit

Include as much of this as possible:

- Full title exactly as printed or cataloged.
- Year, edition, volume, issue, proceedings title, patent number, or archive collection identifier.
- Creator, editor, conference, journal, publisher, or institutional custodian.
- Stable authority link: DOI, Internet Archive, HathiTrust, Google Books, Open Library, Google Patents, USPTO, library record, or finding aid.
- Rights note: public domain, public scan, restricted access, unknown, or contributor-supplied scan.
- Why it matters: concepts, equations, diagrams, patents, historical context, Tesla-era overlap, or field-language value.
- Known defects: missing pages, low-quality OCR, duplicate edition, damaged scan, incomplete plate, or uncertain title page.

If you provide a local file, keep its original filename visible and include where it came from. A checksum is ideal once the file enters the repository.

## Processing Path

Every serious source should move through these gates:

1. Registered source lead.
2. Custody confirmed through a public scan, local public-domain file, official patent record, or archive finding aid.
3. OCR or text extraction created and preserved.
4. Page, chapter, lecture, article, or patent-section map created.
5. Concept, glossary, quote, formula, and figure candidates extracted.
6. Source reader and source overview generated with verification warnings.
7. Scan checks promote exact quotations, formulas, and diagrams.
8. Concept pages, math pages, visual galleries, and comparison pages receive source-grounded updates.

Until step 7, formulas and OCR passages remain candidate material. This archive deliberately avoids pretending damaged OCR is verified mathematics.

## What Not To Do

Do not paste long copyrighted text into an issue. Do not submit a modern paraphrase as if it were Steinmetz's wording. Do not mix Tesla, Wheeler, Dollard, or ether-field interpretation into the source-custody layer. Those comparisons may become useful later, but the first job is always to know exactly what Steinmetz wrote, where, and in which edition.

## Why This Matters

The archive is becoming useful because it is open and careful at the same time. Source acquisition is the hinge between those two virtues. The more complete the custody trail, the stronger every future concept page, equation page, diagram, glossary entry, hidden gem, and interpretive boundary becomes.
